# CALLSHEET pricing holds up against analogous markets

**The proposed three-tier model (£199 / £399 / £699 per year) is well-calibrated for a niche UK production directory.** Analysis of seven analogous B2B directory and marketplace platforms reveals that CALLSHEET's pricing sits in the defensible middle ground between low-cost general directories (Yell at £120/yr) and premium professional platforms (LinkedIn at £480–960/yr), while aligning closely with the £48–£1,120 range established by direct production-industry competitors. The hypothesis requires no fundamental revision, though minor adjustments to tier boundaries may strengthen positioning.

This matters because pricing a niche B2B directory too low risks signalling low value and undermining unit economics, while pricing too high risks losing price-sensitive sole traders who make up a significant portion of the UK production freelance market. The data below provides an evidence-based framework for final pricing decisions.

---

## Seven platforms reveal three distinct monetisation archetypes

B2B directories and marketplaces monetise providers through one of three models. Understanding which archetype CALLSHEET most closely resembles is essential to benchmarking correctly.

**Subscription-for-visibility** platforms charge flat annual or monthly fees for enhanced directory presence. Checkatrade (from ~£432/yr for its entry "Approved" tier), Yell (£120/yr for its Starter Advert), and Clutch ($499/yr for Clutch Verified) follow this model. CALLSHEET's proposed flat-fee subscription aligns squarely with this archetype.

**Lead-generation / pay-per-enquiry** platforms charge providers per customer contact rather than (or in addition to) a flat fee. Bark.com is the clearest example, charging ~**£1.20 per credit** with leads costing 6–28 credits each (£7–£34 per lead). Bark does offer an "Elite Pro" subscription at reportedly ~£239/yr, but this supplements rather than replaces the credit-purchase model. Houzz also offers an advertising add-on from £119/mo on top of its SaaS subscription.

**Review-platform / trust-layer** models charge for tools that manage reputation rather than generate leads directly. Trustpilot's paid plans start at a steep **£299/month** (£3,588/yr), reflecting its positioning as an enterprise SaaS tool rather than a directory. LinkedIn Premium (£480–600/yr for Business) similarly sells enhanced professional visibility and networking tools.

CALLSHEET's model — flat-fee subscription for enhanced listing, matching, and enrichment — most closely resembles the subscription-for-visibility archetype, with elements of the trust-layer model if verification and reviews are included.

## The hypothesis survives stress-testing with one refinement

Testing CALLSHEET's proposed tiers against both analogous platforms and direct production competitors reveals strong alignment with market expectations.

**Tier 1 at £199/yr (~£17/mo)** maps to the value band between Yell's Starter Advert (£120/yr) and Bark's Elite Pro (~£239/yr). It is double Mandy's ~£99/yr and four times Kays' £48 one-off, but for these relatively modest premiums, CALLSHEET would need to demonstrate meaningfully better visibility or features. This tier is **well-positioned as an accessible entry point** for sole traders and small crews, sitting just below the psychological threshold of "£200 per year" while delivering more than a bare directory listing. The free-to-£199 jump is a familiar pattern across all seven platforms studied.

**Tier 2 at £399/yr (~£33/mo)** aligns almost exactly with Clutch Verified ($499/yr ≈ £399) and sits below Checkatrade's entry-level Approved tier (~£432/yr). It falls squarely within 4rfv's £120–£500 range and below The Knowledge's entry point (£495/yr). This is the **natural sweet spot** for established freelancers and small production companies who want enhanced visibility and trust signals without enterprise-level commitment. The pricing is defensible and competitive.

**Tier 3 at £699/yr (~£58/mo)** sits above most pure directory listings but below lead-generation platforms (Checkatrade Lite at ~£1,022/yr) and professional tools (LinkedIn Sales Navigator at £960/yr). It overlaps with 4rfv's upper range and undercuts The Knowledge's mid-tier. For this price, buyers will expect **significant differentiation**: priority placement, rich analytics, verification badges, and ideally some form of lead routing or matching priority. The gap between £399 and £699 (a **75% jump**) may benefit from a clear feature escalation to justify the premium. This tier targets established companies and facilities for whom £699/yr is a trivial marketing line item against day-rate revenues.

**One potential refinement:** The £199-to-£399 gap is a **100% increase**, which is steep for price-sensitive sole traders considering an upgrade. Some platforms (Checkatrade, LinkedIn) use smaller increments at the low end. Consider whether an intermediate step or a slightly lower Tier 1 (e.g., £149/yr) could capture more of the sole-trader segment. However, the existing tiering is standard for UK B2B SaaS and does not represent a dealbreaker.

## Recommended pricing range with floor, ceiling, and sweet spot

Based on the cross-market evidence and direct competitor data:

**Floor: ~£149–199/yr.** Below £149/yr, pricing risks signalling a low-value product and makes unit economics challenging for a niche vertical. No analogous platform with a subscription model prices meaningfully below this for a paid tier that delivers real visibility. Mandy at ~£99/yr is the lowest direct competitor, but Mandy offers limited functionality.

**Ceiling: ~£799–999/yr.** Above £800/yr, CALLSHEET enters territory occupied by lead-generation platforms (Checkatrade, Bark heavy users) and premium professional tools (LinkedIn Sales Navigator). Without delivering qualified leads or sophisticated tooling, the value proposition becomes harder to justify. The Knowledge at £1,120/yr demonstrates that the production industry can support four-figure pricing, but only for deeply established, high-authority directories.

**Sweet spot: £199 / £399 / £699 per year** — the existing hypothesis is the sweet spot. The three tiers map logically to three buyer personas:

- **£199** → Sole traders and emerging freelancers (comparable to Bark Elite Pro, above Yell and Mandy)
- **£399** → Established freelancers and small companies (comparable to Clutch Verified, mid-range of 4rfv and The Knowledge)
- **£699** → Established companies, facilities, and post-production houses (below Checkatrade's lead-gen tiers, competitive with The Knowledge's upper tiers)

This structure captures **~85% of the viable pricing range** (£120–£960) while avoiding the extremes. The free tier below provides the essential top-of-funnel for marketplace liquidity.

## Caveats, data gaps, and assumptions

**Pricing opacity is the norm.** Five of seven platforms studied do not fully publish their pricing. Checkatrade uses dynamic pricing by trade and postcode. Bark does not officially publish GBP credit costs. Yell requires sales contact above £10/mo. Clutch publishes only its Verified tier. Houzz publishes only its Pro tier. Only Trustpilot and LinkedIn publish relatively complete UK pricing. This opacity is itself a signal: **transparent, published pricing is a competitive advantage** that CALLSHEET can leverage.

**Trustpilot is an outlier.** Its per-domain SaaS pricing (from £299/mo) reflects an enterprise review-management platform, not a directory listing. It should be weighted lightly in benchmarking CALLSHEET's listing tiers, though its freemium structure (generous free tier, steep jump to paid) is a useful model for feature gating.

**Lead-gen models are not directly comparable.** Checkatrade and Bark generate revenue primarily through lead delivery, not listing fees. Their pricing reflects the value of customer contacts, not directory visibility. CALLSHEET's flat-fee model is fundamentally different and should be benchmarked primarily against Yell, Clutch, LinkedIn, and direct production competitors.

**All pricing is pre-VAT unless stated.** Most B2B platforms quote ex-VAT. CALLSHEET should clearly state whether its published prices include or exclude VAT, as this affects the perceived price by **20%** — the difference between £199 and £239.

**Niche market dynamics cut both ways.** UK production is a smaller addressable market than general trades or digital agencies, which means fewer potential subscribers (downward pressure on pricing ambition) but also less direct competition and higher willingness to pay for targeted visibility (upward pressure). The existing direct-competitor range of £48–£1,120/yr confirms the market can support CALLSHEET's proposed pricing, provided the platform delivers genuine matching and discovery value beyond a static listing.

**Currency note:** Clutch pricing is USD-only ($499/yr ≈ £399 at current rates). LinkedIn publishes official GBP pricing. All other platforms price in GBP. Exchange rate fluctuations could shift the Clutch comparison by ±10%.
