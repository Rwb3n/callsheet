# UK production directory competitor pricing analysis for CALLSHEET

**The UK production directory market generates between £15–20M annually across all platforms, yet remains dominated by legacy incumbents charging £120–£1,120/year with outdated technology and modest traffic.** This creates a clear opening for CALLSHEET to enter at a competitive price point with a modern platform. The primary competitor 4rfv charges £120–£500/year for listings on a 25-year-old ASP.NET site; The Knowledge charges £495–£1,120/year on a WordPress theme; Mandy charges ~£99/year but targets individual crew rather than companies. No competitor offers a genuinely modern, full-featured directory experience below £400/year, and none provide the analytics, verification, or matching capabilities that a 2026 platform should deliver. CALLSHEET has a pricing window to undercut incumbents at launch while delivering significantly more value.

## 4rfv.co.uk: the primary incumbent charges £120–£500 with limited features

4rfv is a Belfast-based broadcast, film, and TV directory operated by **Flagship Media Group Ltd** (NI030646), a family-run publishing business incorporated in 1996. The site claims **12,500+ company listings** and 110,000+ unique visitors (historically ABC-audited). It runs on legacy ASP.NET/IIS infrastructure that appears largely unchanged for years, with no self-service e-commerce — all upgrades require contacting sales by phone or email.

**Listing tiers are straightforward.** A free submission form exists at `/submitsite.aspx` collecting basic company details (name, activity, contact, address, phone, email, website, single category). These free listings appear in the directory with a stock placeholder graphic, company name, and town — no logo, no description, no additional information. The lowest paid tier is the **Basic Listing at £120/year**, which adds website and email links to the contact details. The **Company Profile at £395/year** is the main upgrade: it includes a company logo (linked to website), a dedicated description page with **1,000 characters** of text, up to 6 product images, promotional video embedding, additional website links, and multi-category listing at £75 per extra category. At the premium end, **Sponsored Category positions** cost **£300 (3rd place), £400 (2nd place), or £500 (1st place)** per year, guaranteeing top-three placement within a specific category with logo and summary text.

**Advertising revenue is a significant separate stream.** 4rfv sells display advertising independently of listings: **leaderboard banners at £390/4 weeks**, MPU ads at £290/4 weeks, daily news email banner ads at £390/4 weeks, dedicated email shots at £600 each, video hosting at £100/year, and equipment marketplace listings at £150/month. The daily news email reaches 7,000+ industry professionals, and a strip of ~10 small banner ads appears at the bottom of every page. A separate rate card exists for their BFV (Broadcast Film and Video) publication.

**Key vulnerability:** 4rfv's manual sales process, stock placeholder images on free listings, dated design, and limited self-service functionality represent the weakest user experience of any incumbent. The site's strength is its longevity, SEO authority (claims ~70% of its categories rank in Google's top 10), and established base in Northern Ireland and UK broadcast.

## The Knowledge commands the highest prices in the market at £495–£1,120/year

The Knowledge (theknowledgeonline.com), self-described as **"the industry bible for film and TV professionals,"** is the most premium-positioned UK production directory. First published as a print directory in **1986**, it is now owned by **Media Business Insight (MBI)**, itself acquired by **GlobalData Plc** (LSE-listed) in June 2022. The directory claims **15,000+ entries across 500 categories**, 80,000 registered users, and 21,500–25,000 monthly unique visitors.

**Four clearly defined tiers structure the pricing.** The **Basic tier is free** and provides a minimal presence: bottom-tier search placement, 1 category, 3 credits listed, and no logo, description, photos, or links. The **Enhanced tier starts from £495/year** and adds a logo/headshot, 150 words of searchable description, website and social links, 2 categories, and 25 credits — appearing in third-tier search results. The **Enhanced Plus at £650/year** adds 1 showreel video, up to 5 photos, 3 categories, 35 credits, and moves to second-tier search placement. The **Premier tier at £1,120/year** delivers top-tier placement plus a carousel feature at the top of category pages, up to 20 showreels, 50 photos, 300 words of text, 5 categories, 75 credits, custom colour branding on the profile page, and a news section. The "from" prefix on all pricing suggests rates may vary by category or negotiation.

**The primary value driver is search ranking.** The Knowledge's four-tier search hierarchy — where Premier listings appear first, Enhanced Plus second, Enhanced third, and Basic last — creates a powerful upgrade incentive. Buyers searching the directory are shown paid listings first, making the ranking position the core monetization lever.

**Searcher access is completely free**, requiring no registration. Free registered accounts enable saving searches, shortlisting suppliers, and using in-platform messaging. This frictionless buyer experience is critical to the platform's value proposition to listed suppliers.

**Multiple additional revenue streams supplement listing fees.** Production Intelligence (PI), a leads service tracking upcoming productions with key contacts, costs **£545/year** for single-user access. The annual print directory sells for **£98 per copy** and reaches 24,000 professionals. Display advertising (MPU, banner) is sold on-site and in the print edition via direct enquiry (no public rate card). A weekly newsletter reaches **29,000 subscribers**. Parent company MBI also operates the Broadcast Awards, Screen Awards, and The Media Production and Technology Show.

**Key vulnerability:** Despite its heritage and premium positioning, The Knowledge's monthly traffic of ~25,000 uniques is relatively modest. The site runs on a Listeo WordPress theme — a commercial off-the-shelf directory template — suggesting limited custom development. Its "from" pricing and phone-based sales process may deter smaller suppliers.

## Mandy.com targets individuals at ~£99/year but employers use it free

Mandy.com is fundamentally different from 4rfv and The Knowledge: it is a **crew and talent marketplace** rather than a company directory. Owned by **Backstage LLC** (US, TA Associates-backed) since 2021, it shares infrastructure with Backstage and StarNow. It claims **2.4–2.9 million registered members** globally, with 100,000+ paying subscribers and 6,500+ new jobs posted monthly.

**The pricing model is simple: free Standard and paid Premium.** Free users can create profiles and appear in the talent database, but the **critical gate is the ability to apply to paid jobs** — free users can only access unpaid "Opportunities" (student and charity projects). **Premium costs approximately £17/month or £99/year** (a ~52% annual discount), though Mandy deliberately does not publish pricing publicly — figures are only revealed during the signup flow. US pricing is lower at ~$7.95/month or $50/year, suggesting regional pricing.

**Premium unlocks job applications, enhanced visibility, and analytics.** Paid features include: applying to all paid jobs, unlimited photo/video/document uploads via Media Locker, boosted search position in employer results, profile image featured to employers, "who viewed your profile" analytics, and access to the Employer Directory for networking. Free profiles have limited media uploads, no search boost, and no profile view analytics.

**Employers pay nothing.** Posting jobs, searching the talent database, using the applicant tracking system, messaging candidates, and scheduling auditions are all free. This one-sided monetization — charging the supply side while keeping the demand side free — is central to Mandy's model and generates significant user resentment (Trustpilot rating of **3.4 stars**, with many complaints about the "pay to play" dynamic).

**Contact details are never publicly visible** on any tier. All communication flows through Mandy's in-platform messaging system, which protects user privacy but prevents direct external contact — a design choice CALLSHEET should evaluate carefully.

**Key vulnerability:** Mandy's acquisition by Backstage has caused profile migration issues, interface changes, and declining UK-specific focus. With only 16 UK employees (down from 19 in 2020) and American directors now running the company from Burbank, the platform is increasingly US-centric. Its individual-focused model also doesn't serve B2B supplier discovery well.

## Kays remains a quiet force at just £48 for a permanent listing

An important finding: **Kays is very much alive.** Now publishing its **42nd Edition (2026)**, the KAYS UK Production Manual is an 800+ page annual contact guide operated by KAYS UK PRODUCTION MANUAL LIMITED (company 02683069, incorporated 1992). It operates across print, website (kays.co.uk), and a mobile app.

The pricing is strikingly simple and low: **a listing costs £48 (£40 + VAT) as a one-off charge, not an annual fee.** This single payment places the listing in the printed manual, website, and app. The printed manual itself sells for **£99**. Website access requires registration. The app offers geo-radius search filters, favourites, and a jobs board. While Kays doesn't have the tier complexity of The Knowledge or 4rfv, its one-time pricing model and multi-platform presence (print + web + app) make it an accessible entry point for UK production professionals.

**Kemps has evolved into Screen Global Production.** The historically significant international counterpart to Kays was rebranded from Kemps to KFTV.com in December 2012, and then to Screen Global Production in February 2025. It is owned by **MBI** (the same parent as The Knowledge), covering **17,000+ companies across 150+ countries**. Pricing is bespoke and enquiry-based, with Silver, Gold, and enhanced profile tiers plus display advertising — no public rate card exists.

**BECTU does not operate a crew directory.** Despite its role as the primary UK media union with 40,000+ members, BECTU offers no publicly searchable member directory. Membership fees range from **£120–£350/year** based on income bands. BECTU's "Early Bird" service provides production bulletins (not crew listings) to members. This confirms there is no union-operated directory competing with commercial platforms.

## International benchmarks show similar pricing structures

**ProductionHub** (US, founded 1999, 150,000+ profiles) charges **$6.99/month for a Basic profile** after a 30-day free trial, with Featured profiles at **$699–$1,099/year** depending on category count (6, 12, or 18 categories). Featured profiles appear at the top of search results with 14–18× more profile views. Employers search free but pay **$75–$165 per job posting**. A unique "response credit" system gates individual lead responses for Basic users, incentivizing upgrade.

**Stage 32** (global, 1M+ members) operates as a networking and education platform rather than a traditional directory. The free tier includes profiles, forums, and job board access. The **Writers' Room subscription costs $29–$39/month ($348–$468/year)**, providing weekly live webcasts with executives, open writing assignments, and a 3,000-hour content vault. Significant transactional revenue comes from script services ($35–$149 each) and education products. Stage 32's model demonstrates the potential of layering education and services revenue on top of a free networking base.

## Companies House financials reveal a small but viable market

**Media Business Insight (The Knowledge's parent) is the only company that discloses revenue: £14.57M for the year ending December 2024.** This figure covers all MBI brands — The Knowledge, Broadcast, Screen International, KFTV/Screen Global Production, Production Intelligence, events, and awards. The Knowledge's share is likely £2–4M based on its relative size within the portfolio. MBI has total assets of **£16.72M** and net assets of £5.87M with a 65% debt ratio. It employs approximately 85 staff across all brands. As a GlobalData subsidiary, it has institutional backing and stable finances.

**Flagship Media Group (4rfv's parent) is a micro-entity with turnover classified under £1M.** Despite employing **58 people** (likely across multiple publications and services beyond 4rfv alone), it has net assets of just **£110,570** with a precarious **94% debt ratio**. Total assets have contracted from ~£3M in 2021 to £1.71M in 2024. The company is family-controlled by the Carstairs family, with student-age family members among the directors. This financial profile suggests 4rfv operates on tight margins with minimal investment capacity.

**The Mandy Network Ltd shows signs of post-acquisition restructuring.** Now a subsidiary of US-based Backstage, it has just **16 UK employees** and saw its cash position collapse from £990K (2023) to **£40K (2024)** — likely reflecting intercompany transfers to the American parent. It files small company accounts, so turnover is undisclosed. Third-party estimates place combined Backstage/Mandy global revenue at ~$11.7M.

## Pricing gaps and differentiation opportunities for CALLSHEET

**The £150–£400/year zone is underserved for feature-rich B2B listings.** 4rfv's Basic (£120) offers almost nothing beyond contact details. 4rfv's Company Profile (£395) offers reasonable features but on a dated platform. The Knowledge doesn't offer a meaningful paid tier below £495. This creates a clear pricing opportunity for CALLSHEET to deliver a full-featured listing — logo, description, portfolio, links — at the **£150–£250/year** range, undercutting The Knowledge by 50%+ while significantly outperforming 4rfv's Basic tier.

**No UK competitor offers meaningful analytics or data-driven features.** Only Mandy provides basic "who viewed your profile" tracking at its premium tier. None of the UK B2B directories (4rfv, The Knowledge, Kays) offer any profile analytics, lead tracking, enquiry attribution, competitor benchmarking, or dashboard insights. CALLSHEET could differentiate strongly by building analytics into even its mid-tier offering — a feature set that is standard in modern SaaS platforms but completely absent from this market.

**Verification and trust signals are a wide-open opportunity.** No UK production directory offers meaningful verification. Mandy has a basic credit verification system. 4rfv and The Knowledge have no trust badges, verified reviews, insurance verification, BECTU membership indicators, or quality certifications. In an industry where trust is paramount (production companies need reliable suppliers under deadline pressure), a robust **verification and trust framework** — verified company details, insurance status, union membership, client endorsements — could be a defining feature that no competitor offers at any price.

**Self-service, instant activation is not available anywhere.** Every UK incumbent requires manual contact, phone calls, or email exchanges to upgrade listings. 4rfv directs all enquiries through contact forms. The Knowledge's "from" pricing and phone-based sales suggest negotiation. Kays' website requires registration to browse. CALLSHEET should offer **instant self-service signup and upgrade** with transparent, published pricing — a modern expectation that the market completely fails to meet.

**The willingness-to-pay ceiling appears to be approximately £500–£600/year for standard UK production suppliers**, based on 4rfv's Company Profile at £395 and The Knowledge's Enhanced at £495 being the most commonly purchased tiers. The Knowledge's Premier at £1,120 is likely purchased only by larger suppliers seeking maximum visibility. For a premium tier with genuinely differentiated features (analytics, verification, priority matching), CALLSHEET could potentially command **£400–£600/year** — but the launch strategy should emphasize value.

**No competitor combines directory, matching, and intelligence in one platform.** The Knowledge sells Production Intelligence (upcoming production leads) as a separate £545/year product. 4rfv publishes daily news emails separately from its directory. Mandy's job board is disconnected from its directory function. CALLSHEET has the opportunity to integrate **supplier discovery, production matching, and market intelligence** into a single platform — collapsing multiple subscriptions into one value proposition.

## Recommended pricing architecture for CALLSHEET V1

Based on competitor data, market positioning, and the gap analysis, the following three-tier structure is recommended for launch:

**Free Tier — "Listed"** should be genuinely useful, not a hollow placeholder. Include: company name, basic contact info, 1 category, 50-word description, and visibility in search results (ranked below paid tiers). The goal is to build directory volume rapidly — The Knowledge's 15,000 listings and 4rfv's 12,500 listings demonstrate that critical mass requires a functional free tier. Free listings should be good enough that companies claim them, creating the inventory that attracts buyers.

**Tier 1 — "Professional" at £199/year (or £19/month)** should be the volume tier that undercuts The Knowledge's Enhanced (£495) and outperforms 4rfv's Company Profile (£395). Include: full logo, 300-word description, up to 10 portfolio images, 1 showreel video, website and social links, 3 categories, basic profile analytics (monthly views and search impressions), and a verified-business badge (Companies House check). At £199, this tier is **60% cheaper than The Knowledge Enhanced** while offering more features, making it an easy decision for price-sensitive suppliers who currently pay 4rfv £120 for a bare-bones listing.

**Tier 2 — "Premium" at £399/year (or £39/month)** should match or exceed the value of The Knowledge's Premier (£1,120) at roughly a third of the price. Include: everything in Professional, plus unlimited portfolio media, up to 5 showreels, 10 categories, priority search placement, featured carousel/spotlight placement, advanced analytics dashboard (enquiry sources, competitor comparison, search ranking insights), insurance and credential verification badges, a direct enquiry button, and early access to production matching leads. The **£399 price point** sits precisely in the gap between 4rfv's Company Profile (£395) and The Knowledge's Enhanced (£495), offering substantially more value than either.

**Tier 3 — "Partner" at £699/year (or £69/month)** is an optional enterprise tier for larger suppliers and facilities houses. Include: everything in Premium, plus dedicated account manager contact, custom profile branding, multiple team member profiles under one company, priority matching for production enquiries, integrated production intelligence alerts, and optional display advertising add-ons. This prices below The Knowledge's Premier (£1,120) while delivering features no competitor offers.

**Advertising should be a separate revenue stream from day one.** Based on 4rfv's rate card (£290–£600 per unit per 4-week cycle), CALLSHEET should plan for display advertising, sponsored category placements, newsletter sponsorship, and potentially featured email introductions — pricing these at market rates once traffic justifies the model.

**Launch discount strategy:** Consider offering the first year of the Professional tier at **£99** (matching Mandy's annual price point — a psychologically familiar figure in this market) to drive rapid adoption, reverting to £199 at renewal. This creates urgency, builds the paid listing base, and gives suppliers a low-risk reason to switch from incumbents.

## Conclusion

The UK production directory market is ripe for disruption. Incumbents rely on decades of heritage and SEO authority but deliver poor user experiences on outdated technology at premium prices. **4rfv operates on a 25-year-old ASP.NET platform with a 94% debt ratio; The Knowledge runs on a WordPress theme; Mandy is drifting toward US-centricity with declining UK headcount.** None offers analytics, verification, self-service activation, or modern matching capabilities. The total addressable market is meaningful — MBI's £14.57M revenue across its brand portfolio, combined with 4rfv's sub-£1M operation and Mandy's global scale, suggests UK production directory and intelligence spending exceeds £15M annually. CALLSHEET's optimal entry point is a transparent, self-service model at £199–£399/year for paid tiers, with a genuinely useful free tier, built-in analytics, and verification features that no competitor provides at any price. The key strategic insight is that **search ranking and visibility are the primary monetization levers** — every successful competitor gates placement position behind payment. CALLSHEET should replicate this proven model while wrapping it in a modern, data-driven platform that makes the legacy directories feel obsolete.
